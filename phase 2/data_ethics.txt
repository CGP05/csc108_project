1.  Where the data came from: My data source was posted on Kaggle by a user named "a1233biology" on April 3rd 2020. 
    However, the Kaggle page does not state who "collected this data and for what purpose".

2.  Who is in the data and who is not: The dataset contains 7 different categories of data per observation/individual 
    (their age, sex, bmi, the number of children they have, if they smoke or not, if the region of Canada that they live in is 
    northeast, northwest, southeast, or southwest, and their total amount of claim charges for this health insurance plan) 
    from 1339 Canadian patients. This dataset is a sample rather than the entire population of people with health insurance in Canada, 
    and does not contain data of people who are not Canadians with health insurance.

    Also, it is important to note that even though the Kaggle page says that this is Canadian data the 4 region titles are usually used more
    for the United States than for Canada.

3.  How the findings could be used: I believe that this dataset (as well as other data sets that are very similar to this) 
    could be used to inform both public policy as well as private sector business strategy. For example, the function percent_high_claims() 
    calculates that approximately 0.52% of the indivudial claims in this data, which could be used for private sector analysts or public 
    to investigate for possible fraud or if they simply have much greater than average medical issues.
